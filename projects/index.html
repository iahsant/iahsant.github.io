research

Soft Actuator Design for Underwater Operation

Soft fluidic actuators are usually designed with structural models that ignore the surrounding water. Underwater, however, fluid loading changes how they bend, how much pressure they need, and how much force they deliver. My research brings fluid–structure interaction into the design loop, using surrogate models to optimize actuator geometry for water.

Modeling and Prediction of Continuum Robots

Continuum robots have nonlinear dynamics that physics-based models capture only in part. Learned models fill this gap but tend to drift over long rollouts. My research combines the two, anchoring learned dynamics to the robot's equilibrium behavior so that predictions stay stable beyond the training data.

Bio-Inspired Sensing for Underwater Manipulation

Underwater vision often fails at the moment of grasping because of turbidity, glare, and occlusion. Weakly electric fish solve a similar problem by sensing distortions in a self-generated electric field. My research builds this active electrosense into soft grippers to detect objects before contact.

Fault-Tolerant Robot Learning

Learned robot policies assume their actuators behave as they did in training. Wear and damage break this assumption during deployment. My research estimates how much actuation a robot has left and uses that estimate to recover performance without retraining the policy.

World Models for Robot Swarms

Collective behavior emerges from many agents acting on local information. At the Max Planck Institute of Animal Behavior, I build compact world models for swarms of small camera-equipped robots. These models run onboard each robot as spiking neural networks and support decentralized control.

Modeling Fish Behavior with Virtual Reality

Virtual reality lets us show fish a controlled social partner and record how they respond. I use system identification to build interpretable models of how fish follow a virtual fish. The goal is a controller that lets a virtual fish steer real fish in closed loop.

Collaborative Work

Fish Sensorimotor Modeling

Fish swimming couples neural, muscular, and hydrodynamic processes. With colleagues, I build interpretable models that link muscle activity to body motion in freely swimming fish. We then transfer these models to a robotic fish.

Telemetry for Freely Swimming Fish

Recording muscle activity in freely swimming fish needs small, waterproof electronics. We built a 16-channel EMG telemetry system for this purpose. From its signals, we decode the fish's body pose and the surrounding flow.

Multifunctional Soft Sensing

Soft bodies in flow can harvest energy from their own deformation. We study whether the same piezoelectric transducers can also sense the body's shape. This would give soft swimmers proprioception without extra sensors.

research

My research develops methods for soft bodies that operate underwater. Soft actuators are compliant and safe around delicate objects, but the surrounding water loads, deforms and damps them in ways that structural models ignore. I work across the pipeline, from designing actuators with fully coupled fluid–structure interaction, to predicting how continuum bodies move over long horizons, to sensing objects before contact when vision fails.

Research Areas

Research Focus

Designing soft actuators for water

Soft fluidic actuators are usually designed with structural FEM, which neglects the fluid loading acting on them underwater. I built a two-way external fluid–structure interaction (FSI) optimization framework that trains non-parametric regression surrogates on a design-of-experiments dataset and searches a parameterized geometry space. The FSI optimum reaches similar bending at 30% lower pressure than the FEM optimum while bending 46% more. In system tests it gives a 58% higher retention force in grasping and 24.8% faster swimming in a fish-like body.

Predicting continuum robot motion

Multi-step prediction for continuum robots breaks down under actuation distribution shift, where errors accumulate and rollouts drift or diverge. I proposed a hybrid model that anchors predictions to a learned equilibrium prior through a contractive update and learns the remaining one-step mismatch with a feature-lifted residual model that is linear in parameters. On 200-step rollouts of a tendon-driven 3D continuum arm under stronger and faster actuation than in training, it reduces backbone RMSE by 26% and tip RMSE by 27% and stays stable on every tested trajectory. A soft-tail hardware test confirms the gain under actuation-frequency shift.

Sensing before contact underwater

Turbidity, glare and gripper occlusion make vision unreliable during underwater grasping, and soft grippers usually have no cue before contact. I instrument an octopus-inspired soft gripper with active electrosense, a discrete electrode layout that measures perturbations of an applied electric field in water. Simulation and tank experiments with a conductive sphere show structured, object-dependent changes in the multi-electrode voltage readout across excitations of 5 to 20 V and frequencies from 1 mHz to 1 kHz. This gives a pre-contact cue for guiding approach and closure when vision fails.

research

My research addresses the design, modeling, and sensing of soft robotic systems operating underwater. Hydrodynamic loading alters the deformation, energetics, and stability of soft bodies in ways that structural models and in-air assumptions fail to capture. My work spans fluid–structure interaction-based design optimization of soft actuators, stable long-horizon prediction for continuum robots, and pre-contact sensing for underwater soft manipulation.

Research Focus

Fluid–Structure Interaction-Based Design of Soft Actuators

Structural finite element analysis, the standard tool for designing soft fluidic actuators (SFAs), neglects the hydrodynamic loads acting on the actuator underwater and therefore biases the predicted pressure–deformation response. We present a geometric optimization framework that places two-way external fluid–structure interaction (FSI) simulation inside the design loop. Non-parametric regression (NPR) surrogates trained on a design-of-experiments dataset are searched over a parameterized design space, and the resulting optimum is verified with high-fidelity coupled simulation. The FSI-optimized actuator reaches comparable bending at 30% lower pressure than the FEM-optimized design while increasing the bending angle by 46%. System-level tests report a 58% higher retention force in grasping and a 24.8% higher forward velocity in a fish-like swimmer.

Stable Long-Horizon Prediction for Continuum Robots

Multi-step motion prediction for continuum robots suffers from error accumulation under actuation distribution shift, which distorts the predicted steady-state response and destabilizes rollouts. We propose a hybrid equilibrium-anchored residual-learning framework for a tendon-driven 3D continuum arm. An equilibrium prior learned from static data is enforced through a contractive update that pulls each prediction toward the equilibrium estimate, and a feature-lifted residual model, linear in parameters, recovers the remaining transient dynamics from trajectory data. Over 200-step rollouts under stronger and faster actuation than seen in training, the method reduces backbone and tip position RMSE by 26% and 27%, respectively, and remains stable on all tested trajectories. A soft-tail hardware experiment under actuation-frequency shift further validates the approach.

Pre-Contact Electrosensing for Underwater Soft Grippers

Vision-based perception degrades during underwater manipulation because of turbidity, glare, and occlusion by the gripper itself, and soft grippers generally lack an onboard cue prior to contact. This work investigates active electrosense as a pre-contact sensing modality for underwater soft grippers. An octopus-inspired gripper is instrumented with a discrete electrode layout, and perturbations of an applied electric field are recorded as multi-channel sensing voltages. Simulation and tank experiments with a suspended conductive sphere show structured, object-dependent voltage changes relative to empty-water baselines for excitation amplitudes of 5–20 V and frequencies from 1 mHz to 1 kHz.

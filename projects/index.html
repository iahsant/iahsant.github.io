research

Soft Actuator Design for Underwater Operation

Soft fluidic actuators are usually designed with structural models that ignore the surrounding water, although hydrodynamic loading changes how they deform, how much pressure they need, and how much force they transmit. I develop design methods that account for this coupling, combining fluid–structure interaction simulation with surrogate modeling and optimization so that actuators are shaped for the medium they work in. The resulting designs carry over to soft grippers and swimming bodies.

Modeling and Prediction of Continuum Robots

Continuum and soft robots have highly nonlinear, high-dimensional dynamics that analytical models capture only in part, while purely data-driven models drift over long horizons. I work on hybrid models that combine physical structure, such as the robot's equilibrium behavior, with learned residual dynamics, so that predictions remain stable and accurate when actuation moves beyond the training distribution.

Bio-Inspired Sensing for Underwater Manipulation

Underwater vision is limited by turbidity, glare, and occlusion, most of all at the moment of grasping. Inspired by weakly electric fish, I explore active electrosense as an onboard sensing modality for soft grippers, using perturbations of a self-generated electric field to detect objects before contact.

Fault-Tolerant Robot Learning

Learned robot policies assume that actuators behave as they did during training, an assumption that breaks under wear, damage, or degradation. I study how robots can estimate the remaining effectiveness of their actuators during deployment and use that estimate to recover performance without retraining the original policy.

World Models for Robot Swarms

Collective behavior emerges from many agents acting on local information. At the Max Planck Institute of Animal Behavior, I develop compact world models for swarms of small camera-equipped robots, implemented as spiking neural networks that run onboard each robot and support decentralized control.

Modeling Fish Behavior with Virtual Reality

Virtual reality makes it possible to present fish with controlled social stimuli and record how they respond. I use system identification to build interpretable models of how fish follow a virtual conspecific, with the aim of understanding the behavior and closing the loop so that a virtual agent can steer real fish.

Collaborative Work

Fish Sensorimotor Modeling

Fish swimming arises from tightly coupled neural, muscular, and hydrodynamic processes. With colleagues, I work on interpretable models that link muscle activity to body kinematics in freely swimming fish and transfer them to robotic fish.

Telemetry for Freely Swimming Fish

Recording muscle activity in freely swimming fish requires compact, waterproof electronics. We developed an EMG telemetry system and learning-based decoding that recover body pose and the surrounding flow conditions from muscle signals.

Multifunctional Soft Sensing

Soft bodies in flow can harvest energy from their own deformation. We study whether the same piezoelectric transducers used for harvesting can also serve as proprioceptive sensors, estimating the body's shape without dedicated sensing elements.

<div class="card research-card mb-4"><div class="row no-gutters"><div class="col-md-7 research-media dark"><video autoplay loop muted playsinline preload="metadata"><source src="{{ '/assets/video/tmech_research_clip.mp4' | relative_url }}" type="video/mp4"></video></div><div class="col-md-5 d-flex align-items-center"><div class="card-body"><div class="research-text"><h5 class="card-title"><a href="{{ '/assets/pdf/Tanveer_TMECH2026_accepted_with_supplementary.pdf' | relative_url }}">Soft Actuator Design for Underwater Operation</a></h5><p class="card-text">Soft fluidic actuators are usually designed with structural models that ignore the surrounding water. <div class="card research-card mb-4"><div class="row no-gutters flex-md-row-reverse"><div class="col-md-7 research-media dark"><video autoplay loop muted playsinline preload="metadata"><source src="{{ '/assets/video/rss_research_clip.mp4' | relative_url }}" type="video/mp4"></video></div><div class="col-md-5 d-flex align-items-center"><div class="card-body"><div class="research-text"><h5 class="card-title"><a href="https://anchored-rollouts.github.io/">Modeling and Prediction of Continuum Robots</a></h5><p class="card-text">Continuum robots have nonlinear dynamics that physics-based models capture only in part. Learned models fill this gap but tend to drift over long rollouts. My research combines the two, anchoring learned dynamics to the robot's equilibrium behavior so that predictions stay stable beyond the training data.</p></div></div></div></div></div>
<div class="card research-card mb-4"><div class="row no-gutters"><div class="col-md-7 research-media"><img src="{{ '/assets/img/research/electrosense_fig1.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Electrode layout and simulated electric field of the electrosensing gripper"></div><div class="col-md-5 d-flex align-items-center"><div class="card-body"><div class="research-text"><h5 class="card-title"><a href="https://openreview.net/forum?id=rdWW5xPrhN">Bio-Inspired Sensing for Underwater Manipulation</a></h5><p class="card-text">Underwater vision often fails at the moment of grasping because of turbidity, glare, and occlusion. Weakly electric fish solve a similar problem by sensing distortions in a self-generated electric field. My research builds this active electrosense into soft grippers to detect objects before contact.</p></div></div></div></div></div>
<div class="card research-card mb-4"><div class="row no-gutters flex-md-row-reverse"><div class="col-md-7 research-media dark"><video autoplay loop muted playsinline preload="metadata"><source src="{{ '/assets/video/fdr_clip.mp4' | relative_url }}" type="video/mp4"></video></div><div class="col-md-5 d-flex align-items-center"><div class="card-body"><div class="research-text"><h5 class="card-title">Fault-Tolerant Robot Learning</h5><p class="card-text">Learned robot policies assume their actuators behave as they did in training. Wear and damage break this assumption during deployment. My research estimates how much actuation a robot has left and uses that estimate to recover performance without retraining the policy.</p></div></div></div></div></div>
<div class="card research-card mb-4"><div class="row no-gutters"><div class="col-md-7 research-media"><video class="research-square" autoplay loop muted playsinline preload="metadata"><source src="{{ '/assets/video/rpi_swarm_clip.mp4' | relative_url }}" type="video/mp4"></video></div><div class="col-md-5 d-flex align-items-center"><div class="card-body"><div class="research-text"><h5 class="card-title">Action-Conditioned World Models for Robot Swarms</h5><p class="card-text">Collective behavior emerges from many agents acting on local information. At the Max Planck Institute of Animal Behavior, I build compact world models for swarms of small camera-equipped robots. Each robot runs its model onboard and uses it for decentralized control.</p></div></div></div></div></div>
<div class="card research-card mb-4"><div class="row no-gutters flex-md-row-reverse"><div class="col-md-7 research-media"><img src="{{ '/assets/img/research/lilab_fishvr.jpg' | relative_url }}" alt="A fish following a virtual fish in a virtual reality setup"></div><div class="col-md-5 d-flex align-items-center"><div class="card-body"><div class="research-text"><h5 class="card-title">System Identification of Fish Following Behavior</h5><p class="card-text">Fish adjust their heading in response to the movements of nearby fish. At the Max Planck Institute of Animal Behavior, I use system identification to model how zebrafish respond to heading changes of a virtual fish. These interpretable models form the basis of a controller that lets a virtual fish steer real fish in closed loop.</p></div></div></div></div></div>

<div class="card research-card mb-4"><div class="row no-gutters"><div class="col-md-7 research-media"><img src="{{ '/assets/img/research/biorobot_arx.jpg' | relative_url }}" alt="ARX workflow from fish EMG to robotic fish"></div><div class="col-md-5 d-flex align-items-center"><div class="card-body"><div class="research-text"><h5 class="card-title"><a href="https://bio-to-robot.github.io/">Fish Sensorimotor Modeling</a></h5><p class="card-text">Fish swimming couples neural, muscular, and hydrodynamic processes. With colleagues, I build interpretable models that link muscle activity to body motion in freely swimming fish. We then transfer these models to a robotic fish.</p></div></div></div></div></div>
<div class="card research-card mb-4"><div class="row no-gutters flex-md-row-reverse"><div class="col-md-7 research-media"><img src="{{ '/assets/img/research/emg_hardware.jpg' | relative_url }}" alt="EMG electrode placement and telemetry hardware"></div><div class="col-md-5 d-flex align-items-center"><div class="card-body"><div class="research-text"><h5 class="card-title"><a href="https://emg-fish-kinematics.github.io/">Telemetry for Freely Swimming Fish</a></h5><p class="card-text">Recording muscle activity in freely swimming fish needs small, waterproof electronics. We built a 16-channel EMG telemetry system for this purpose. From its signals, we decode the fish's body pose and the surrounding flow.</p></div></div></div></div></div>
<div class="card research-card mb-4"><div class="card-body"><div class="research-text"><h5 class="card-title">Multifunctional Soft Sensing</h5><p class="card-text">Soft bodies in flow can harvest energy from their own deformation. We study whether the same piezoelectric transducers can also sense the body's shape. This would give soft swimmers proprioception without extra sensors.</p></div></div></div>

<div class="card research-card mb-4"><div class="row no-gutters"><div class="col-md-7 research-media dark"><video autoplay loop muted playsinline preload="metadata"><source src="{{ '/assets/video/tmech_research_clip.mp4' | relative_url }}" type="video/mp4"></video></div><div class="col-md-5 d-flex align-items-center"><div class="card-body"><div class="research-text"><h5 class="card-title"><a href="{{ '/assets/pdf/Tanveer_TMECH2026_accepted_with_supplementary.pdf' | relative_url }}">Soft Actuator Design for Underwater Operation</a></h5><p class="card-text">Soft fluidic actuators are usually designed with structural models that ignore the surrounding water, although hydrodynamic loading changes how they deform, how much pressure they need, and how much force they transmit. I develop design methods that account for this coupling, combining <b>fluid–structure interaction simulation</b> with <b>surrogate modeling and optimization</b> so that actuators are shaped for the medium they work in. The resulting designs carry over to soft grippers and swimming bodies.</p></div></div></div></div></div>
<div class="card research-card mb-4"><div class="row no-gutters flex-md-row-reverse"><div class="col-md-7 research-media dark"><video autoplay loop muted playsinline preload="metadata"><source src="{{ '/assets/video/rss_research_clip.mp4' | relative_url }}" type="video/mp4"></video></div><div class="col-md-5 d-flex align-items-center"><div class="card-body"><div class="research-text"><h5 class="card-title"><a href="https://anchored-rollouts.github.io/">Modeling and Prediction of Continuum Robots</a></h5><p class="card-text">Continuum and soft robots have highly nonlinear, high-dimensional dynamics that analytical models capture only in part, while purely data-driven models drift over long horizons. I work on <b>hybrid models</b> that combine physical structure, such as the robot's equilibrium behavior, with <b>learned residual dynamics</b>, so that predictions remain stable and accurate when actuation moves beyond the training distribution.</p></div></div></div></div></div>
<div class="card research-card mb-4"><div class="row no-gutters"><div class="col-md-7 research-media"><img src="{{ '/assets/img/research/electrosense_fig1.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Electrode layout and simulated electric field of the electrosensing gripper"></div><div class="col-md-5 d-flex align-items-center"><div class="card-body"><div class="research-text"><h5 class="card-title"><a href="https://openreview.net/forum?id=rdWW5xPrhN">Bio-Inspired Sensing for Underwater Manipulation</a></h5><p class="card-text">Underwater vision is limited by turbidity, glare, and occlusion, most of all at the moment of grasping. Inspired by weakly electric fish, I explore <b>active electrosense</b> as an onboard sensing modality for <b>soft grippers</b>, using perturbations of a self-generated electric field to detect objects before contact.</p></div></div></div></div></div>
<div class="card research-card mb-4"><div class="card-body"><div class="research-text"><h5 class="card-title">Fault-Tolerant Robot Learning</h5><p class="card-text">Learned robot policies assume that actuators behave as they did during training, an assumption that breaks under wear, damage, or degradation. I study how robots can <b>estimate the remaining effectiveness of their actuators</b> during deployment and use that estimate to <b>recover performance</b> without retraining the original policy.</p></div></div></div>
<div class="card research-card mb-4"><div class="row no-gutters flex-md-row-reverse"><div class="col-md-7 research-media"><img src="{{ '/assets/img/research/lilab_swarm.jpg' | relative_url }}" alt="Swarm robots with light trails"></div><div class="col-md-5 d-flex align-items-center"><div class="card-body"><div class="research-text"><h5 class="card-title">World Models for Robot Swarms</h5><p class="card-text">Collective behavior emerges from many agents acting on local information. At the Max Planck Institute of Animal Behavior, I develop compact <b>world models</b> for swarms of small camera-equipped robots, implemented as <b>spiking neural networks</b> that run onboard each robot and support decentralized control.</p></div></div></div></div></div>
<div class="card research-card mb-4"><div class="row no-gutters"><div class="col-md-7 research-media"><img src="{{ '/assets/img/research/lilab_fishvr.jpg' | relative_url }}" alt="A fish following a virtual fish in a virtual reality setup"></div><div class="col-md-5 d-flex align-items-center"><div class="card-body"><div class="research-text"><h5 class="card-title">Modeling Fish Behavior with Virtual Reality</h5><p class="card-text">Virtual reality makes it possible to present fish with controlled social stimuli and record how they respond. I use <b>system identification</b> to build interpretable models of how fish follow a virtual conspecific, with the aim of understanding the behavior and <b>closing the loop</b> so that a virtual agent can steer real fish.</p></div></div></div></div></div>

<div class="card research-card mb-4"><div class="row no-gutters"><div class="col-md-7 research-media"><img src="{{ '/assets/img/research/biorobot_arx.jpg' | relative_url }}" alt="ARX workflow from fish EMG to robotic fish"></div><div class="col-md-5 d-flex align-items-center"><div class="card-body"><div class="research-text"><h5 class="card-title"><a href="https://bio-to-robot.github.io/">Fish Sensorimotor Modeling</a></h5><p class="card-text">Fish swimming arises from tightly coupled neural, muscular, and hydrodynamic processes. With colleagues, I work on <b>interpretable models</b> that link muscle activity to body kinematics in freely swimming fish and <b>transfer them to robotic fish</b>.</p></div></div></div></div></div>
<div class="card research-card mb-4"><div class="row no-gutters flex-md-row-reverse"><div class="col-md-7 research-media"><img src="{{ '/assets/img/research/emg_hardware.jpg' | relative_url }}" alt="EMG electrode placement and telemetry hardware"></div><div class="col-md-5 d-flex align-items-center"><div class="card-body"><div class="research-text"><h5 class="card-title"><a href="https://emg-fish-kinematics.github.io/">Telemetry for Freely Swimming Fish</a></h5><p class="card-text">Recording muscle activity in freely swimming fish requires compact, waterproof electronics. We developed an <b>EMG telemetry system</b> and learning-based decoding that recover <b>body pose</b> and the surrounding <b>flow conditions</b> from muscle signals.</p></div></div></div></div></div>
<div class="card research-card mb-4"><div class="card-body"><div class="research-text"><h5 class="card-title">Multifunctional Soft Sensing</h5><p class="card-text">Soft bodies in flow can harvest energy from their own deformation. We study whether the same <b>piezoelectric transducers</b> used for harvesting can also serve as <b>proprioceptive sensors</b>, estimating the body's shape without dedicated sensing elements.</p></div></div></div>
